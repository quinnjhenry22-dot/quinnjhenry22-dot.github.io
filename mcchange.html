Why McChange

Every student has a "someone should fix this."

Maybe it's the line you stand in every week. Maybe it's a service nobody knows exists, or a space on campus that could be so much more.

We've all had that thought. Most of the time it ends there, because it's hard to know who to tell or how to make anyone listen. McChange gives that thought a stage, a mentor, and a real path to the people who can say yes.

This isn't a hypothetical case competition. The problems are ours. The solutions are yours. And the winning idea gets a real meeting, a real budget, and a real follow-up.

How the day works

Three stages. One day.

You'll walk in with an idea and walk out with a sharper one. Here's how the day unfolds.

01Morning

Opening pitches

Your team takes the stage for a short, punchy pitch in front of a panel of four McGill professors. Keep it tight. You have three minutes to make your case and two more to answer their questions.

  • 3-minute pitch, followed by 2 minutes of Q&A
  • Each professor picks the 3 ideas they most want to mentor
  • 4 professors × 3 picks = 12 finalist teams
02Midday

Mentorship and refinement

This is where the magic happens. Finalists spend a few hours with the professor who chose them, turning a good pitch into a concise, proper proposal. It's your chance to work through anything your first pitch didn't quite answer.

Feasibility Costs Who it serves Who at McGill needs to approve it

Win or not, every finalist team leaves with real guidance from McGill faculty.

03Afternoon

Final pitches

The 12 finalists pitch again, this time to a brand-new panel. These are the people who shape McGill's decisions and budgets. Once every team has presented, the judges choose the winner.

  • New panel of donors, board members and faculty and department heads
  • A refined proposal, not just a pitch
  • One winner announced at the close of the day
What the winner gets

Not a trophy. A real shot at change.

The winning team doesn't just get applause. They get the three things every good idea needs to become real.

01

A seat at the table

A formal meeting with the McGill office, faculty, or SSMU body that could actually implement your idea.

02

Money to start

A small implementation budget, funded by our sponsors and donors, to help you take the first real steps.

03

A follow-up that counts

A check-in later in the year where your team shares its progress. It keeps everyone accountable, including us.

What kind of ideas are you looking for?

Real solutions to real problems inside McGill. Think student services, campus spaces, accessibility, sustainability, academic life, or anything else you've looked at and thought "this could be better." The best ideas are specific, doable, and serve real people on campus.

Do I need a finished plan to apply?

No. You need a clear problem and a strong idea for solving it. The mentorship stage exists to help finalists work through costs, feasibility and approvals with a professor's guidance.
